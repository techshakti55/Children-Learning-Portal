## 1. Counting World

Objectives: Recognise natural and whole numbers. Explain where zero belongs.

Prerequisites: Count objects from 1.

We use natural numbers to count: 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on. In this portal, natural numbers start at 1.

Whole numbers are 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. Zero tells us there are no objects.

A number can belong to more than one family. Every natural number is also a whole number. Zero is whole but is not natural. Neither family has a greatest number.

### nt-l1-ex1: An empty basket has no apples. Which number shows this?

1. Count the apples: there are none.
2. Use 0 to show no apples.
3. 0 is a whole number. It is not a natural number here.

Answer: 0

### nt-l1-ex2: Which families contain 7?

1. 7 is a counting number, so it is natural.
2. Whole numbers include every natural number.

Answer: 7 is both natural and whole.

## 2. Pair Up

Objectives: Identify even and odd whole numbers. Explain why zero is even.

Prerequisites: nt-l1, Count in groups of two.

An even whole number can be split into pairs with nothing left over. An odd whole number leaves one object without a partner.

Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9.

Zero is even: zero objects make zero pairs, with nothing left over. Also, 0 = 2 × 0. A whole number is either even or odd; it cannot be both.

### nt-l2-ex1: Are 8 socks even or odd?

1. Make pairs: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8.
2. There are 4 pairs and no sock is left.

Answer: 8 is even.

### nt-l2-ex2: Are 7 counters even or odd?

1. Make 3 pairs: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6.
2. One counter is left without a partner.

Answer: 7 is odd.

### nt-l2-q01

Answer: B

Explanation: 16 ends in 6 and makes 8 pairs with nothing left over.

- C. 47
- D. 58

Explanation: 47 ends in 7, so it is odd.

### nt-l2-q03

## 3. Prime Detectives

Objectives: Find positive factors using equal rows. Identify prime and composite numbers.

Prerequisites: nt-l1, Understand equal groups and simple multiplication.

A positive factor of a whole number divides it exactly, with no remainder. The positive factor pairs of 6 are 1 × 6 and 2 × 3. Its positive factors are 1, 2, 3 and 6.

A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. A composite number is a whole number greater than 1 with more than two positive factors.

1 has only one positive factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. 0 is also neither prime nor composite: these two groups start above 1. Every positive whole number divides 0 exactly, so 0 does not have exactly two positive factors.

2 is prime: its factors are 1 and 2. It is the only even prime. Every other even whole number greater than 2 has 1, 2 and itself as different factors.

Number families overlap. For example, 2 is natural, whole, even and prime. Prime does not mean odd: 2 is prime, while 9 is odd and composite.

### nt-l3-ex1: Is 7 prime or composite?

1. Try equal rows: 1 × 7 works.
2. 2 and 3 leave a remainder; reversing the rows gives 7 × 1.
3. The complete positive factor list is 1, 7. There are exactly two.

Answer: 7 is prime.

### nt-l3-ex2: Is 9 prime or composite?

1. Make rectangles: 1 × 9 and 3 × 3.
2. List each positive factor once: 1, 3, 9.
3. There are three positive factors, which is more than two.

Answer: 9 is composite.

## 4. Number Neighbours (optional enrichment)

Objectives: Order integers on a number line. Identify co-prime pairs using common factors. Identify twin primes.

Prerequisites: nt-l1, nt-l2, nt-l3, List all positive factors of small positive numbers.

Optional challenge: you can finish the core topic after Lesson 3. Try this lesson when you feel ready.

Integers are ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ... . They include negative whole-number values, zero and positive whole numbers. Fractions such as 1/2 are not integers.

Picture a number line: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3 from left to right. A number farther right is greater. Every whole number is an integer, but a negative integer is not a whole number.

First find common factors: these are positive factors shared by two positive whole numbers. For 8 and 9, the factors are 1, 2, 4, 8 and 1, 3, 9. Their only common factor is 1.

Two positive whole numbers are co-prime when their only common positive factor is 1. They do not both need to be prime: 8 and 9 are composite but co-prime. No HCF method is needed; compare the factor lists.

Twin primes are two prime numbers with a difference of 2, such as 3 and 5. Both numbers must be prime. 7 and 9 differ by 2, but 9 is composite, so they are not twin primes.

### nt-l4-ex1: Which is greater: -2 or 1?

1. Find -2 and 1 on the labelled number line: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3.
2. 1 is to the right of -2.

Answer: 1 is greater than -2.

### nt-l4-ex2: Are 3 and 5 co-prime and twin primes?

1. The positive factors of 3 are 1, 3. The positive factors of 5 are 1, 5.
2. Their only common factor is 1, so they are co-prime.
3. Both numbers are prime and 5 - 3 = 2, so they are also twin primes.

Answer: 3 and 5 are both co-prime and twin primes.
